What We Treat

Spotted Lanternflies in Blairstown, NJ

The spotted lanternfly is an invasive planthopper that has spread aggressively across New Jersey since its arrival from Asia. Warren County properties — especially those with tree-of-heaven, maples, willows, and grape vines — are prime feeding grounds for this destructive pest.

Lanternflies are active from May through November, with peak adult populations from July through October. They feed in large groups on tree bark and plant stems, excreting sticky honeydew that coats decks, patios, cars, and outdoor furniture. The honeydew promotes sooty mold growth and attracts wasps and other insects.

Left untreated, lanternfly populations explode year over year. They lay egg masses on any smooth surface — trees, rocks, outdoor furniture, vehicles — each containing 30-50 eggs that hatch the following spring. Control requires eliminating adults, destroying egg masses, and treating feeding sites.

How We Treat It

Our Lanternfly Control Process

01
Dinotefuran Systemic + Bifenthrin Contact
We use dinotefuran as a systemic treatment applied to tree bark — it absorbs into the tree and kills lanternflies as they feed on sap. Bifenthrin contact spray is applied to surfaces where lanternflies congregate for immediate knockdown of active populations.
02
Targeted Application Areas
We treat tree bark with banding applications, vegetation surfaces where lanternflies feed and rest, and hardscape areas where they gather. We also identify and destroy egg masses on trees, stones, and structures during fall and winter inspections to reduce next year's population.
03
May Through November, Peak July-October
Treatment runs through the full lanternfly lifecycle — from early nymph emergence in May through adult die-off in November. Peak treatment frequency is July through October when adult populations are largest and most damaging. Egg mass removal continues through winter.
Safety

Safe for Your Family and Pets

Pet Safety

Contact spray is safe for pets once dry (30-60 minutes). Systemic bark treatments are applied directly to tree trunks and are not accessible to pets. We do not use broadcast spray — all applications are targeted to specific trees and surfaces where lanternflies are present.

Kid Safety

Targeted tree and surface treatments mean children are not exposed to treated areas during normal yard activity. Once contact spray dries, treated surfaces are safe. Systemic treatments absorb into bark and are not present on exterior surfaces children would touch.

Wildlife Safety

Our approach is target treatment — not broadcast spray. We treat specific trees and surfaces where lanternflies feed, avoiding flowering plants and pollinator habitat. Dinotefuran systemics work through the tree's vascular system, affecting only insects that feed on treated plants.
