## WEBSITE COPY — "Our Approach" Section

**Smarter Pest Control, Backed by Science**

Most pest control companies show up on a fixed schedule — every month, same spray, same routine, whether pests are active or not. At Pest'Em, we do things differently. We practice Integrated Pest Management (IPM), which means we start by identifying exactly what pest you're dealing with and understanding how it lives, feeds, and reproduces. From there, we work through the most responsible solutions first: sealing entry points, removing the conditions pests need to thrive, and using targeted treatments only where they're needed. Broad-spectrum pesticide applications are a last resort, not a first instinct. The result is pest control that actually solves the problem instead of just masking it with chemicals.

We also time our treatments using a method called Growing Degree Days — GDD for short. Instead of guessing when pests will show up based on the calendar, we track accumulated heat data throughout the season to predict exactly when specific pest populations are hatching, emerging, or hitting their peak. Pest biology runs on temperature, not dates, and a warm March can shift everything by weeks. By monitoring GDD thresholds for each pest we manage, we treat during the narrow window when it will do the most good. That means fewer applications, less product in your environment, and better results than the old "spray and pray" approach.

This combination of IPM and GDD-based scheduling is what separates Pest'Em from conventional pest control. For you, it means a healthier home or business with fewer chemicals around your family, employees, or customers. For the environment, it means reduced pesticide load in our New Jersey waterways and soils. And because we're treating at the biologically optimal time with the right method, you'll actually see fewer pests — not more. It's pest control that's smarter for everyone.

## DETAILED MONTHLY BREAKDOWN

### FEBRUARY
**Overwintering Invaders (BMSB, Cluster Flies, Lady Beetles)**
- Trigger: Sunny days, exterior walls >50°F
- Treatment: Interior crack-and-crevice dust (CimeXa/delta dust) in wall voids, vacuum visible aggregations
- IPM: Seal gaps at window frames, door sweeps, utility penetrations, soffit vents

**Rodents (Mice)**
- Trigger: Year-round; peak interior intrusion Nov–Mar
- Treatment: Check/refresh exterior bait stations, re-set interior snap traps
- IPM: Inspect for frost heave gaps at sill plate. Remove bird feeders within 10 ft of structure

### MARCH
**Subterranean Termites**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 100–150 GDD₅₀ | Dates: ~Mar 25–Apr 15
- Treatment: Inspection and sales window. Liquid barrier (fipronil) or bait stations for active infestations
- IPM: Fix grading/drainage, repair gutters, remove wood-soil contact

**Deer Tick (Adults resumed questing)**
- GDD Base: 40°F | Trigger: 50–100 GDD₄₀ | Dates: Mid-March through May
- Treatment: 1st perimeter acaricide spray (bifenthrin/permethrin) along lawn-woodland edges, stone walls, shaded beds
- IPM: 3-ft wood chip barrier at lawn-woods edge, remove leaf litter, keep grass mowed short at transition zones

### APRIL
**Carpenter Ants**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 100–400 GDD₅₀ | Dates: Early April; swarmers late April–May
- Treatment: Direct nest injection (fipronil foam), non-repellent barrier (Termidor/Alpine WSG), gel bait indoors
- IPM: Identify and correct moisture sources. Trim branches touching structure

**Spotted Lanternfly (Egg Hatch)**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 150–200 GDD₅₀ | Dates: Late April to mid-May
- Treatment: Band trees before hatch. At hatch: dinotefuran bark spray + bifenthrin perimeter
- IPM: Scout for egg masses from Feb; scrape into alcohol. Map Ailanthus on property

### MAY
**Deer Tick (Nymphs — HIGHEST PRIORITY)**
- GDD Base: 40°F | Trigger: 500–700 GDD₄₀ | Dates: Mid-May through June (PEAK late May–mid June)
- Treatment: **2nd perimeter acaricide — most important tick treatment of the year.** Add tick tubes at edge areas
- IPM: Re-mow edges, clear leaf litter, send Lyme disease awareness to customers

**American Dog Tick**
- GDD Base: 40°F | Trigger: 400–800 GDD₄₀ | Dates: May through mid-July
- Treatment: Covered by the tick perimeter spray. Extend zone into open grasslands/meadows if present

**Mosquitoes (Season Start)**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 400–500 GDD₅₀ | Dates: Late May
- Treatment: Begin 21-day barrier spray (bifenthrin/lambda-cyhalothrin). Bti granules for standing water
- IPM: Walk property — clogged gutters, birdbaths, pot saucers, tarps, abandoned tires, tree holes

### JUNE
**Yellowjackets & Wasps**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 600–800 GDD₅₀ | Dates: June = optimal treatment window
- Treatment: Ground nests: dust (Tempo/DeltaDust) at night. Aerial nests: pressurized residual spray
- IPM: Inspect eaves/soffits early — queen-only nests in May need no chemical treatment

### JULY
**SLF (Late Instars → Adults)**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 1000–1600 GDD₅₀ | Dates: 4th instars July; first adults late July
- Treatment: Re-apply systemic bark spray if heavy pressure. Spot-treat aggregations on structures
- IPM: Replace sticky bands with circle traps (reduces bird bycatch)

**Mosquitoes (Peak)**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 1000–2000 GDD₅₀ | Dates: July through mid-September
- Treatment: Continue 21-day barrier spray cycle. Re-apply Bti to standing water
- IPM: Re-inspect for new breeding sites after summer rain events

### AUGUST
**Yellowjackets (Peak Aggression)**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 2000–2500 GDD₅₀ | Dates: August–September
- Treatment: Emergency ground nest calls — treat at dusk, full PPE. Colonies at 1,000–5,000 workers
- IPM: Fallen fruit cleanup, covered trash lids, commercial accounts: clean dumpster pads

**SLF (Adults, Egg Laying begins)**
- GDD Base: 50°F | Trigger: 1800–2500 GDD₅₀ | Dates: August through frost
- Treatment: Perimeter spray on south/west-facing walls. Systemic treatment on Ailanthus trap trees
- IPM: Begin scouting for egg masses. Grey-brown putty, ~1 inch long, on smooth surfaces

### SEPTEMBER
**Overwintering Invaders — EXCLUSION WINDOW (most important)**
- Trigger: Nighttime lows first hit 50°F (mid-September Warren County)
- Treatment: Residual barrier spray on south/west-facing exterior walls BEFORE pests enter. Reapply if rain within 48 hrs
- IPM: Full exclusion — seal AC units, torn screens, utility penetrations, install door sweeps, screen gable vents 1/16" mesh

**Deer Tick (Fall Adults)**
- GDD Base: 40°F | Trigger: ~2800 GDD₄₀ accumulated | Dates: Late September through November
- Treatment: 3rd perimeter acaricide application. Same edge habitats
- IPM: Advise treated clothing for leaf cleanup. Discuss deer fencing for high-pressure properties

### OCTOBER
**Rodents (Fall Invasion — Critical Entry Window)**
- Trigger: Nighttime lows drop below 40°F consistently
- Treatment: Deploy exterior bait stations along foundation, near garage doors, outbuildings. Interior snap traps
- IPM: Full exclusion inspection. Mice enter through 6mm gap. Key spots: AC line penetrations, dryer vents, garage weatherstripping, sill plate junction. Seal with copper mesh + caulk or steel wool + foam

**SLF Egg Mass Scraping**
- No chemical. Manual scraping into alcohol/hand sanitizer. October through March
- Systematic survey of all smooth-barked trees, stone walls, outdoor furniture, vehicles, building surfaces

### NOVEMBER–JANUARY
**Rodents — Ongoing**
- Monthly bait station checks. Document activity. Replace bait. Reinspect exclusion after freeze/thaw

**Termites — Monitoring**
- Check bait stations per manufacturer protocol
- Best season to correct grading, drainage, wood-soil contact issues

## OPERATIONAL NOTES

1. **Track GDD locally** — Use NRCC GDD tools or install local weather station. Warren County accumulates GDD slower than coastal NJ. Belvidere NJ is the closest NOAA station.

2. **Regulatory compliance** — NJ requires 7A/7B licenses. SLF near waterways may need additional buffers. Bti and tick tubes have lower regulatory burden.

3. **Customer-facing version** — The quick reference table can be reformatted as a branded "Your Annual Protection Plan" handout.

4. **Blairstown note** — Rural and spread out = lower residential density but higher tick/wildlife pressure per property. Higher-value per-job area. Consider travel time in pricing.
